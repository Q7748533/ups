About ParcelRate

A shipping-rate reference built on published tariffs instead of guesses.

Where the numbers come from

Every price on this site is parsed directly from the official USPS price files published at usps.com/business/prices. Each table shows its effective date and the exact file it came from. Nothing is estimated, averaged, or carried over from an older price sheet.

Where the zone comes from

A USPS zone is not a region on a map. It is a property of the pair of ZIP codes — the one the parcel enters the mail at, and the one that delivers it. The same destination is Zone 1 from the next town over and Zone 8 from the other coast, so "what zone is California" has no answer. Zone 1 is the local zone, defined as the same NDC as the origin; zones 2 through 9 step outward.

The zone finder on this site reads the USPS Domestic Zone Chart, effective October 1, 2026. The table is downloaded once and searched in your browser — the ZIP codes you type are never sent to us or to USPS.

One case needs care: APO and FPO addresses. USPS does not give them a single zone. For 09012 from New York the chart says Zone 1, except Priority Mail and Ground Advantage under 16 oz., which are Zone 5. Where that happens we price each service at its own zone and say so, instead of quietly using one number for all of them.

What we don't do

  • We don't quote "average" shipping costs. There is no such number — weight, size and distance decide the price.
  • We don't mix price sheets from different years. If a carrier hasn't published a new tariff, we don't show it next to one that has.
  • We don't guess your commercial discount. All figures are published list rates.
  • We don't publish a table we can't parse accurately. An incomplete page is better than a wrong one.

Update cadence

Price files are re-fetched every 12 hours. Last fetch: 2026-10-23T04:35:36Z.

The effective date is not the download date, and it is not the date printed inside the price file either. USPS stamps its CSV files with the day the file was generated — the October 2026 files are stamped September 14 but took effect October 4. So we read the effective date from the USPS price change notices instead, and every table shows that date.

Current price change: Time-Limited 2026 Price Change, effective October 4, 2026 through January 17, 2027. USPS has announced this as a time-limited change, so the figures on this site expire on that date. We will publish the next set when USPS does.

Coverage

Currently: USPS domestic services only. FedEx and UPS publish their tariffs as multi-hundred-page PDFs whose tables don't extract reliably; adding them is planned but not done. We'd rather ship one carrier correctly than three badly.
