Flat Rate vs regular pricing — the break-even weight

A Flat Rate box costs the same no matter how far it goes. Regular Priority Mail charges by weight and zone. This table shows the exact weight at which regular pricing becomes the more expensive of the two.

How to read this

Each cell is the weight at which weight-based Priority Mail first costs as much as or more than that Flat Rate option. Below that weight, regular pricing is cheaper. Above it, the Flat Rate box wins.

"never" means regular pricing does not reach that flat rate at any published weight tier up to 70 lb — the flat rate is always the more expensive option.

What this actually means

  • Short zones favour regular pricing. On Zone 1–2 you need several pounds before a Small Flat Rate box pays for itself, because local weight-based rates are already cheap.
  • Long zones favour Flat Rate almost immediately. On Zone 8 the 1 lb regular rate is already above the Small Flat Rate price, so Flat Rate wins from the first pound.
  • Heavy and far is where Flat Rate shines. That is the combination USPS prices most aggressively on the weight-based side.
Size still matters. Flat Rate boxes have fixed dimensions. If your item doesn't fit, the comparison is moot. And every Flat Rate option caps at 70 lb.

All figures are USPS published list rates. Commercial or negotiated prices can be lower. Nothing here is an estimate.
